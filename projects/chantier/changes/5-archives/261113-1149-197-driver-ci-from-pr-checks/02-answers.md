# Answers

- Q: Is the forge's list the only source even when the project's rules say the project has no CI? — Advisor (Fable, answering in the user's place on the user's word of 2026-11-12): Yes: the claim always comes from the forge's list at the pushed head; the rules only say how to judge what the list shows, and a list that contradicts the rules is said in a `note`. One look is cheap, and a stale rule is exactly how a running check gets reported as absent.
- Q: When may a Driver conclude "no CI", given that checks appear some time after the push? — Advisor (Fable, answering in the user's place on the user's word of 2026-11-12): Only after a second look, taken after a short bounded wait (about a minute) following the push, still lists no check; an empty first look, or a watch that ends at once, is not a conclusion. A forge registers its checks a few seconds late, and that is the likely cause of the three false reports.
- Q: Must the claim be tied to the head? — Advisor (Fable, answering in the user's place on the user's word of 2026-11-12): Yes: the Driver confirms the PR's head on the forge is the head it pushed before it reads the checks, and its note names that head. Checks listed for an older commit prove nothing about the pushed one.
- Q: Which Drivers does the rule bind? — Advisor (Fable, answering in the user's place on the user's word of 2026-11-12): Every Driver that pushes (CR, stack, release), since the rule sits in the common `CI:` bullet. A stack or a release that reports "no CI" wrongly costs as much as a CR.
- Q: What does the PR body's CI box say when the forge lists no check? — Advisor (Fable, answering in the user's place on the user's word of 2026-11-12): The box stays, checked, and reads `No CI: no check listed at <sha7>`; it is never deleted and never written "CI green". The template already asks for "None" rather than a missing section, and the SHA keeps the claim checkable.
- Q: Does the Verifier's card change? — Advisor (Fable, answering in the user's place on the user's word of 2026-11-12): No: a CI line in the description is already a claim the Verifier judges, and it reads the forge's list itself to judge it. The existing rule (a claim with no proof is `NOT VERIFIED`) covers it.
- Q: Should the CLI refuse a `handback ready` that has no CI reading? — Advisor (Fable, answering in the user's place on the user's word of 2026-11-12): No. The CLI runs no forge command and knows no role; the card carries the rule.
- Q: Is an eval check part of this change? — Advisor (Fable, answering in the user's place on the user's word of 2026-11-12): One row in `evals/SCORECARD.md`, checked by hand on the real runs (a Driver's "no CI" follows a checks call made after the push); no automated audit. The defect was found in a real run and must be looked for in the next one, but an audit is more than the fix needs.
- Q: The one-minute wait meets rule 8 of concepts.md (no sleep, no polling loop). If the Driver's other work ends in under a minute after an empty first look, what then? — Chief of Staff (on the user's word of 2026-11-12, no user impact): one bounded wait, once, until a minute after the push, then the second look: a named exception in the Driver card's CI: bullet, which rule 8 points to. Never a loop; a second empty look concludes "No CI".
