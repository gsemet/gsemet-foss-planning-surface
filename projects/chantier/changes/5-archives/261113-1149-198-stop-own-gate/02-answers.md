# Answers

- Q: Is the named way a rule in the card alone (stop the process you started, by its id), or a means of the CLI? — Advisor (Fable, answering in the user's place on the user's word of 2026-11-12): A means of the CLI, on the `gate` command, named in the card. Ending the `gate` command by its id leaves the suite running in its own process group, and the next long gate waits for it for up to an hour; only the CLI knows the group, from its lock.
- Q: Does this respect the rule that a new CLI tool comes only from measured behaviour? — Advisor (Fable, answering in the user's place on the user's word of 2026-11-12): Yes: it comes from a recorded incident that cost a whole CR, it adds no command (a way of `gate`, which is already the CLI's), and it reuses the stop that `cancel` already has. The gate and its lock are the batch's frame, which is the CLI's scope.
- Q: When may a Driver stop its own gate? — Advisor (Fable, answering in the user's place on the user's word of 2026-11-12): Only when the run's result can no longer count (the head is about to move: a `rebase:` note, a change that needs a round) or on the steering session's `stop`; never because the gate is slow. A gate that would still be a valid pass is worth more finished than restarted.
- Q: On a `rebase:` note received mid-gate, does the Driver stop the gate or let it finish? — Advisor (Fable, answering in the user's place on the user's word of 2026-11-12): It stops it, then rebases and runs the gate at the new head. The gate is alone in the repository, so a run whose result is void holds every other CR's gate back for nothing.
- Q: What does a stopped gate leave in the record? — Advisor (Fable, answering in the user's place on the user's word of 2026-11-12): A gate event and a last log line that say `stopped` at its head, distinct from a failure, and no pass; the CR's status, notes, worktree and folder are untouched. A stop read as a red gate would send a later reader to hunt a defect that is not there.
- Q: May the means stop another CR's gate, or be used when no gate runs? — Advisor (Fable, answering in the user's place on the user's word of 2026-11-12): It stops only a gate the lock names for the CR given, and says so without stopping anything otherwise (another CR's gate: refused; none running: nothing to stop, no error). A Driver must never be able to end a neighbour's suite.
- Q: Should the CLI itself refuse `cancel` from a Driver? — Advisor (Fable, answering in the user's place on the user's word of 2026-11-12): The CLI knows no role, so not by role; but `cancel` without `--epoch` is refused (exit 2) with a line that names the gate's stop, since every steering command already carries its epoch (scorecard C9) and only `cancel` accepts its absence. The card's sentence alone would not have stopped an agent that never read it as a rule; every steering use in the cards, the scenario and the evals already passes the epoch.
- Q: Who else gets the rule: the Verifier, the stack and release Drivers? — Advisor (Fable, answering in the user's place on the user's word of 2026-11-12): The sentence on `cancel` and the stop go in the common "Your means" of driver.md, so every Driver has them; the Verifier's card is unchanged (it runs a gate only when a claim needs it, and lets it end). The incident is a Driver's, and a worker has no reason to stop a gate.
- Q: Is a mistaken cancel made easier to undo by this change? — Advisor (Fable, answering in the user's place on the user's word of 2026-11-12): No, out of scope. `restore` already brings a trashed CR back, and the fix is to make the mistake impossible, not cheaper.
- Q: The CLI cannot stop a Driver from stopping a neighbour's gate; is the card's "own CR only" rule enough? — Chief of Staff (on the user's word of 2026-11-12, no user impact): yes. The CLI knows no role, and a check on the calling worktree would be a guess at who calls; the card's rule holds.
- Q: Does a `stopped` run void an earlier pass at the same head? — Chief of Staff (on the user's word of 2026-11-12, no user impact): yes, as the current rule says: the last gate run at a head decides. A stopped gate means the head is to be proved again.
- Q: A Driver's own gate still waiting for the lock behind another CR: the card tells the Driver to end its own `gate` command by its id? — Chief of Staff (on the user's word of 2026-11-12, no user impact): yes, intended.
- Q: `take`, `pause`, `order done`, `agents set` and others also accept a missing epoch; change them here? — Chief of Staff (on the user's word of 2026-11-12, no user impact): no, this CR changes `cancel` only; the others are filed as their own inbox item.
