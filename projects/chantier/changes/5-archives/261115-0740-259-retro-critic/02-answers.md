# Answers

Grilled with the user on 2026-11-14.

1. **Where the critic cuts.** The Foreman runs the critic itself before it writes `4-batch/retro.md`:
   the file, and so the batch's record in `5-archives/batches/`, holds only the kept points.
   (Chosen over the alternatives: the critic cutting only what the Chief of Staff offers, with
   the full retro kept in the record; or the Chief of Staff rewriting the Foreman's file.) In
   direct mode, where the Chief of Staff writes the retro, it runs the critic the same way.
   Consequence to handle in the specification: a point dropped once leaves no trace, so the
   critic's "more than one occurrence" test reads the earlier batches' records, which hold only
   kept points, and the logs of the CRs cited.
2. **Its cost.** No cap in dollars: the critic is bounded by what it reads, the retro's points
   and the evidence they cite (the logs of the CRs named, the batch's report), nothing else. Its
   cost counts in the batch's cost and shows in `cost`.
3. **Its scope.** Every list of changes the Chief of Staff or the Foreman proposes on its own
   goes through the critic first: a batch's or a release's retro, a report's suggestions, an
   answer to a question such as "are the workers useful?". A change the user asked for does not.

- Spec open points (2026-11-14), the spec's recommendations stand, decided by the Chief of Staff on the user's go to open the batch at once: dropped points in `4-batch/retro-dropped.md` (deleted at close), chat only outside a batch; the critic does not search GitHub issues; 1. the pause's critic run takes the release Driver's points along with the batch's; 2. a single unasked suggestion also goes through the critic; 3. a critic that fails twice: offer nothing that pause, say "retro not checked: <cause>", drafted points on request.
