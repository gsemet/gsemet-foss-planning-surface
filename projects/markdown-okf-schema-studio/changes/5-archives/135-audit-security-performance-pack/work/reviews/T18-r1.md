## Findings

1. **The overrun check compares two different measures, so the abort can thrash**:
   `src/node-services/build/buildQueue.ts:275` and `:292-303`, with
   `src/session/previewManager.ts:338`.
   - `lastBuildMs` comes from `durationOf` = `result.renderMs`: the MyST time alone, without the
     engine load, the worker queue wait, the message copy, the `SharedRenders` join or the rest of
     `renderPreviewDocument`.
   - `elapsed` is the wall-clock time of the whole build.
   - For a small or medium document (`renderMs` 0.5 to 20 ms, the debug log in T18-r1), the
     overrun limit falls to its floor, `debounceMs` = 40 ms. Any build that takes more than 40 ms of
     wall time while a newer edit is due gets aborted: a host busy with the sanitizer or a webview
     patch, or the other document's render ahead in the single worker.
   - Each abort terminates the thread. The replacement needs 53 to 118 ms before it renders anything
     (measured above, more under window-test load). The next build waits for that cold start and
     can be aborted in turn, which kills the spare that is still loading. The limit doubles per
     abort until it covers the cold start.
   - The next successful build then resets `lastBuildMs` to its small `renderMs`, and the cycle
     can start again. The result is wasted thread restarts and a preview that lags while the writer
     types, on exactly the small documents that were fast before.
   - The SwRS-LIFE-039 row "the next pause grows to twice the time the aborted render ran" holds
     for one build only.
   - Required fix:
     - Base the overrun limit on the wall-clock duration of the last completed build. `renderMs` may
       still drive the adaptive debounce. Alternatively, give the overrun a floor that covers a
       thread restart (for example `maxDebounceMs`).
     - Never abort a build whose render has not reached a ready worker yet; that only restarts a
       cold thread.
     - Add a buildQueue test: `durationOf` reports 2 ms, the build takes 60 ms of wall time, a
       newer edit is due at 40 ms, and the build is not aborted.
     - Keep the LIFE-039 text and the verification notes in line with whatever rule results.

2. **Branches of the worker lifecycle have no test**: `src/node-services/renderWorker.ts:200-224`
   (death of a ready worker, the crash counter, falling back after 3) and `:296-316` (the wait for
   a busy thread and the abort of a waiter). The report lists these as open point 4, and LIFE-004
   states them as normative rows but marks them "untested". These are the error paths where a leak
   or a hang would hide. Each test is cheap with `scriptPath` pointing to a stub worker written to a
   temp dir.
   - Required fix: add tests for these 3 cases:
     - A stub that posts `ready`, then calls `process.exit(1)` on `render`. The render rejects with
       "stopped during the render". After 3 renders, `disabledReason` is set and the 4th render runs
       in-process.
     - Two concurrent renders: the second waits (`started` stays 1) and then completes.
     - A waiting render whose signal aborts: it rejects as canceled and never reaches the worker.
   - Testing the idle stop through `idleMs` is optional. Update LIFE-004's verification notes.

3. **`scenarioAlertDropdown`: a worker-induced regression is plausible and is not ruled out**
   (report open point 1). Reading the code, the worker changes timing in two ways that bear on
   "a page blur, then a content patch closes the block editor":
   - (a) The render result now arrives asynchronously from another thread. Page-to-host messages
     (edits, saves, edit-lock releases) are now handled during a render. Before, the synchronous
     MyST render made them wait until it ended, so their order relative to the patch has changed.
   - (b) Finding 1 can abort the render of the NOTE edit and delay its patch by a cold thread
     start. That would land the patch inside the blur window of the click on WARNING.
   - The numbers: 4 of 4 failures with the worker, 1 of 1 pass without it. The base flake rate
     recorded in T6 and T7 is about 1 in 2. That is suggestive (p about 0.06), not conclusive.
   - Required fix:
     - After fixing finding 1, run the trusted real-window suite for this scenario alternately on
       the base (or with workers off) and with the worker: at least 4 runs each, same machine, under
       `WINDOW-TESTS.lock`. Report the counts.
     - If the worker still fails clearly more often, find the ordering that closes the editor, and
       fix it or keep the editor open across the patch. Do not integrate T18 on the strength of
       "known flake" alone.

     - In the CHANGELOG, say that the MyST render moved off the extension host, not that freezes
